FINITE PUBLIC-SOURCE PoS PANEL: COMPLETION METHOD
As of 16 November 2026. Project 1 only. Supersedes use of the preliminary 418-row count as a finished denominator.

Question and estimand
Estimate observed US approval within five, eight and ten years of initial human development in a defined rare-disease discovery panel, then progressively restrict biological/clinical relevance to CMP-002. The population originally sought was eventual approval from an early/pre-dose landmark. Public trial records do not supply a census of preclinical failures or complete lifetime outcomes. The measurable horizon estimand must therefore be kept separate from Needham's 25% eventual-approval assumption.

Scope and sources
The frozen starting inventory contains 518 mapped drug-indication records from an earlier public-registry discovery pass over 48 rare-disease terms. All 449 previously retained or entry-uncertain records receive another individual history review; 69 earlier exclusions remain visible. This is completion of a finite panel, not a claim to cover every rare disease, every historical drug, or all drugs generally. Saved ClinicalTrials.gov API histories, sponsor filings and releases, investigator publications, regulators, and disease-foundation operational trackers support adjudication. The official Drugs@FDA archive and FDA CBER product/annual approval pages provide a separate approval cross-check. Archives that could not be retrieved remain logged; a current product list is not a complete historical withdrawal archive.

Pipeline
1. Resolve molecule/product identity, names, sponsors, and all-indication human history. A later indication, formulation or rebrand does not reset the first-human clock. Healthy-volunteer exposure counts. Distinct molecules sharing a target remain distinct; a generic cell label is not a unique asset.
2. Establish first-human entry as a day, month, year, or bounded interval. Actual study start can precede injection. A first-dose announcement bounds rather than necessarily dates the first dose. Keep registry-start proxies explicitly separate from corroborated initial-human windows. Identify pre-2010 exposure, post-2024 first dosing, never-dosed trials, diagnostics and unrelated products.
3. Determine US target-indication approval and development status independently. Trial termination, completion, licensing, partner withdrawal, or loss of funding does not automatically mean permanent program failure. Non-US approval is not FDA approval. First FDA approval remains an event after a later withdrawal, but withdrawal is retained for commercial and durability analysis. No later disease indication rescues an otherwise failed target program.
4. Normalize researched aliases and cross-check FDA original applications and CBER products. Hits require molecule/indication confirmation; no-hit results are operational negative screens, not proof that every alias or historical CBER record was captured. Preserve this residual ascertainment limitation in the resulting rates.
5. Apply the same nested filters to one underlying ledger: rare panel; genetically defined CNS; genetic medicines within that CNS set; intrathecal protein restoration in neurodevelopmental disease; and the frozen four closest ASOs. Literal target novelty is not inferred from mechanism labels. Unknown novelty prevents a defensible extra novel-target rate.
6. Calculate horizon fractions only among entrants old enough to have the full horizon. A program discontinued before the horizon remains in the denominator. An active or development-status-unknown program also remains, if identity/entry are usable, with no observed FDA approval. Recent entrants are not counted as long-horizon failures. Entry intervals produce certain/possible event counts and a separate maturity-boundary list.

Sensitivity structure
Report corroborated-entry subset, broader reviewed-entry/proxy panel, and uncertain-entry inclusion sensitivity. The last is a deliberate assumption, not evidence that uncertain products qualify. Show 2010–2024 and 2010–2020 entry windows, plus 2015–2020 to emphasize more recent experience. Different horizon denominators mean the displayed fractions need not trace one common cohort survival curve.

Drug-indication records remain auditable. Shared constructs/combinations are also grouped for a cluster sensitivity, using earliest qualifying approval among included original/co-developed indications. Do not choose a lexically convenient indication or the best outcome after seeing results. Earliest-component and latest-component entry clocks are both reported for shared combination clusters; neither silently claims an independent fixed-combination inception date. Confidence intervals, if displayed, describe binomial arithmetic only: they do not account for selected diseases, correlated assets, missing programs or imperfect name linkage.

Pre-dose relevance
Clean novel programs registered but never dosed are retained separately. Zero-enrollment trials of previously exposed drugs are not first-entry attrition. This boundary evidence cannot estimate all-preclinical-to-approval PoS, nor be added ad hoc to a clinical-entry denominator to create a falsely precise preclinical rate.

Interpretation and stopping rule
Every assigned row receives a bounded public-source review, with exact supporting sources and unresolved facts retained. A completed search is not a resolved historical fact. Do not manufacture permanent failures from silence or treat unresolved identities as successes/failures. The finite-panel work ends after complete coverage, reconciliation and sensitivity calculations; further expansion requires a new sampling frame or historical development database. Do not blend overlapping nested rates as independent evidence. The four closest programs explain mechanism and translation risks but are too few and immature to calibrate an eventual approval probability alone.
