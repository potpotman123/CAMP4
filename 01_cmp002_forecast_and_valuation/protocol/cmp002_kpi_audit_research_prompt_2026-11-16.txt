CMP-002 IN SYNGAP1: INDEPENDENT KPI AND PROBABILITY-OF-SUCCESS AUDIT
Research assignment prepared 16 November 2026

PURPOSE

Conduct an extensive, independent, source-verifiable investigation to calibrate the inputs for a worldwide CMP-002 revenue forecast and a separate risk-adjusted valuation. Audit Needham's assumptions using appropriate historical and contemporary comparators. Do not assume CAMP is undervalued, that sell-side forecasts are conservative, or that 25% probability of success is too high. Find evidence that could justify, lower, or raise each assumption.

The user wants to reconstruct what happened to genuinely comparable programs in the past, including failures, rather than apply a broad industry average or select famous successes. Historical information available before first human dosing is particularly important. More recent clinical and commercial experience generally deserves greater relevance, but recent unresolved programs must not be mistaken for successes or failures.

This is a new KPI-calibration project, separate from the existing question about systematic sell-side underforecasting for first-entry rare-disease DMTs. A drug need not be a first DMT to be an excellent comparator for a particular KPI. Do not require historical analyst estimates to include a case here.

Use 16 November 2026 as the initial evidence cutoff. State any later cutoff explicitly. Distinguish a retrospective audit of Needham's 15 October 2026 assumptions using information available then from an updated assessment using subsequently available information. Do not import later clinical results into an earlier as-of judgment.

3. BUILD KPI-SPECIFIC COMPARATOR UNIVERSES

Use a shared master case library with separate, explicitly justified membership for each KPI. No single universal peer ranking and no single first-DMT screen should determine every input. Freeze inclusion criteria before tabulating results; previous outcomes are already known, so do not claim blinding.

For each case document: disease biology, genetic causality and penetrance, loss versus gain of function, dosage restoration versus knockdown, modality/chemistry/mechanism, administration, disease onset and reversibility, target human validation, novelty of platform and target, stage at entry, geography, reimbursement environment, endpoints, and relevance to each KPI. Separate molecular target validation from evidence that the proposed intervention can safely improve clinical function at the treated age.

Candidate families:
- Development risk: genetically defined CNS programs; intrathecal ASOs; gene-expression-restoring approaches; pediatric neurodevelopmental/haploinsufficiency programs. Include approved, failed, discontinued, dormant, active and unresolved candidates.
- Delivery and retention: repeated CNS administration, pediatric specialist-center treatment, anesthesia/procedure burden and caregiver travel.
- Diagnosis and uptake: rare genetically diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders and developmental/epileptic encephalopathies, including symptomatic therapies where commercially informative.
- Price and reimbursement: chronic rare-disease therapies with similar benefit, route, age and payer mix; one-time gene therapies only as explicitly different sensitivities.
- Geographic rollout: matched indication launches across the US, Europe and other relevant markets.

Candidate leads include nusinersen, tofersen, tominersen, BIIB078, CNS ASO programs in Angelman and Dravet, and gene-restoration programs in pediatric CNS disorders. 4. PoS: DESIGN A DEFENSIBLE HISTORICAL COHORT

Primary question: Among programs sufficiently comparable to CMP-002 at the same observed pre-human-data landmark, what fraction eventually obtain first US approval, over what period, and with what uncertainty?

Use drug-indication programs as the unit. Track aliases, acquisitions, reformulations and sponsor transfers to avoid duplication. Multiple trials, doses and analysts are not independent programs. Show sensitivity to correlated assets from a common platform, sponsor or target. Where later indication success follows failure in the original indication, do not count the original indication as successful.

Construct nested cohorts:
1. Broad CNS ASO clinical-entry cohort as an accessible reference, with prespecified date windows.
2. Genetically defined CNS disease subset.
3. Pediatric neurodevelopmental and dosage-restoration subsets, including nearest available mechanistic peers.
4. Exact-stage late-preclinical/cleared-but-not-dosed cohort where a credible denominator can be recovered.
5. Adjacent modalities or diseases as separate supporting strata, not indiscriminately pooled.

Compare narrow biological relevance against sample size and follow-up completeness. If no useful exact-match denominator exists, say so. Do not produce an apparently precise estimate from a handful of handpicked programs.

Entry dates must be observable contemporaneous milestones, not backfilled from eventual success. An all-Phase-I cohort has already survived preclinical development and cannot directly estimate probability from early discovery. A Phase-I-only cohort can still be informative for a cleared program awaiting dosing, with the remaining gap explicit. Discovery-stage attrition estimates cannot be mechanically applied to a program that has already passed those hurdles.

Outcomes and competing explanations:
- First approval: accelerated/conditional versus traditional, indication, geography, date and label restrictions.
- Technical discontinuation: safety, insufficient target engagement, insufficient clinical efficacy, dose/distribution limitations, or manufacturing problems.
- Commercial/strategic discontinuation, financing failure, sponsor failure, or licensing return.
- Active, paused, dormant, missing status, and lost follow-up, each separately defined.
- A negative trial is an event, not necessarily program termination; resumption must be tracked.

For investor-relevant overall approval likelihood, a program abandoned for nontechnical reasons cannot simply be removed as though approval were assured. For biological efficacy questions, analyze technical outcomes separately. Show how classifications change the result.

Avoid the two common biased calculations: approvals divided by all programs when many are still active, and approvals divided only by resolved programs when time to resolution differs. Present raw counts first, follow-up distribution and unresolved share. Estimate fixed-horizon approval incidence (for example 5, 10 and 15 years from entry where supported). Use time-to-event/competing-risk methods only when dates and censoring assumptions warrant them. Treat permanent discontinuation as a competing event, not ordinary independent censoring. Explain remaining informative-censoring problems. Do not equate 'not approved by year 10' with 'will never be approved.'

Report older mature cohorts and newer cohorts separately, with a clearly specified recency sensitivity. Recent cohorts are more technologically relevant but have shorter follow-up. Do not solve that conflict by counting their unresolved programs as successes, deleting them, or assigning arbitrary outcome-driven weights. Use elapsed follow-up from the entry landmark rather than raw calendar years alone.

Provide uncertainty intervals appropriate to the method; a simple binomial interval is insufficient if censoring and dependency materially affect the estimate. If sample sizes are too small, give descriptive evidence and a reasoned range rather than false statistical precision. Hierarchical/Bayesian modeling is optional only if justified by sufficient data, transparent priors and material information gain; ask before extensive fitting.

5. PoS: BRIDGE EMPIRICAL RESULTS TO CMP-002

Use broad published success-rate studies as background priors, not final answers. Verify starting stage, period, denominator, endpoint and definitions. Do not multiply or average overlapping subgroup rates for neurology, rare disease, biologics and genetic targets as though they were independent effects.

Decompose the path into distinct questions: safe CNS delivery; adequate distribution; meaningful target engagement; restoration within a safe dosage range; clinical reversibility at relevant ages; observable benefit on feasible endpoints; confirmatory and regulatory success. This is a causal checklist, not authorization to invent independent numerical probabilities for correlated events.

Investigate both favorable and unfavorable evidence for 25%:
- Favorable possibilities: strong monogenic causal evidence, a remaining functional allele, relevant animal rescue, established delivery experience, objective endpoints, regulatory flexibility, and late-preclinical risks already resolved.
- Unfavorable possibilities: untested platform/target combination in humans, translation and brain distribution, neurodevelopmental timing, dose toxicity, endpoint variability, small populations, recruitment and trial execution, and uncertain relationship between seizure reduction and broader function.

For every claimed adjustment, state the supporting observation, competing explanation, transferability limit and whether numerical calibration is possible. Do not automatically penalize every novel target equally or assume severe unmet need raises biological success probability.

Present: broad benchmark; closest credible empirical cohort; CMP-specific differences; defensible low/base/high range; confidence; and exactly what future evidence would change the estimate. Give both an as-of-Needham assessment and an updated assessment if appropriate. The conclusion may be that 25% is aggressive, defensible, conservative, or presently unresolvable. Do not start by anchoring on a preferred 5%, 10% or 25% answer.

6. AUDIT THE COMMERCIAL KPIs

For each KPI, provide its exact definition, units, denominator, geography, year, Needham value, best comparator evidence, contrary evidence, transferability limits, and proposed scenario range. Reconcile quantities before interpreting differences.

A. Epidemiology and eligibility
Triangulate clinical/genetic studies, claims, diagnosed-patient counts, registries, advocacy censuses and population data. A census of known families is not a population-complete registry. Separate birth incidence, living prevalence, diagnosed prevalence, clinically eligible patients and commercially accessible patients. Audit genotype definitions and VUS treatment. Model age cohorts, survival, new births, age-out rules and continued treatment after adulthood. Avoid applying pediatric prevalence to all ages or assuming every pathogenic variant is equally treatable. Diagnosis prevalence denominators must match the underlying genetic and clinical population.

B. Diagnosis growth
Measure absolute diagnosed patient additions and, where supportable, the proportion of the true population diagnosed. Distinguish new diagnoses from new registry enrollment or expanded advocacy reach. Evaluate genetic-testing adoption, payer access, diagnostic odyssey, laboratory reclassification, clinic capacity and launch-driven testing. Seek comparable prelaunch-to-postlaunch diagnosis trajectories, including cases where patient finding disappointed. Do not assume treatment availability alone produces the observed increase; distinguish secular testing improvements and sponsor efforts. Test whether 50% is plausible and how fast it could be reached.

C. Treatment uptake and market share
Separate label eligibility, reimbursement approval, treatment willingness, initiation, and allocation among competing therapies. Define whether the denominator is all prevalent, diagnosed, eligible, reimbursed or reachable patients. Estimate launch-year and subsequent uptake, not just peak. Account for backlog depletion, incident patients, country launch timing, procedural capacity and competing entrants. An oral seizure therapy may inform family/physician behavior but not intrathecal procedural acceptance. Keep efficacy-dependent uptake scenarios explicit; do not assume human benefit from preclinical results.

D. Adherence, persistence and dosing
Separate never-starting, delayed doses, missed doses, temporary interruption, permanent discontinuation, death and age-out. Use patient-years and actual paid dosing where possible. A combined 75% haircut may conceal several different mechanisms. Do not count discontinuation both in patient retention and again in annual revenue per patient. Audit loading and maintenance regimens, treatment frequency, vial use/wastage, free supply and trial-to-commercial transitions. Treat future CMP-002 dosing frequency as uncertain unless established.

E. Price and gross-to-net
Separate WAC, annualized list cost, per-dose net price and realized net revenue per treated patient-year. Distinguish loading from maintenance, weight-based from fixed dosing, and acquisition cost from hospital/procedure cost. Compare demonstrated clinical benefit and payer mix, not just rarity. Separate rebates, discounts, patient assistance, free supply and FX from adherence. Do not infer precise net prices from list prices or company total sales divided by incomplete patient counts. Distinguish current dollars from future nominal launch/peak dollars; assess the 3% annual escalation independently.

F. Geography
Build the US, EU4 + UK and other material territories explicitly where evidence permits. Prioritize Japan, Canada, Australia and additional Europe for screening; include other territories only with defensible access and pricing assumptions. Model population, diagnosis, genotype/age mix, regulatory timing, reimbursement, net price, distribution and center capacity. Do not equate regulatory approval with funded access. Test Needham's 25%-of-US shortcut against observed matched product-country histories. Missing geography data should yield transparent scenarios, not invented precise country forecasts.

G. Competition, timing and durability
Independently map clinical and potential commercial competitors; first-to-market is a scenario, not guaranteed. Distinguish disease-modifying competitors from symptomatic treatment and possible combination use. Model postapproval launches, switching, persistence, access constraints and changing standards of care. Separate patent expiry, regulatory exclusivity, extensions, actual substitutable competition and loss of market share. Investigate evidence on oligonucleotide competition rather than borrowing an oral generic cliff without qualification. Audit clinical timeline and recruitment comparators so launch assumptions are not disconnected from PoS evidence.

7. TRANSLATE EVIDENCE INTO A COHERENT MODEL

Deliver a geography-by-year forecast structure with diagnosed stocks, new diagnoses, eligible stocks, new starts, continuing treated patients, paid doses and net revenue. Do not construct a finished numeric model using unsupported inputs simply to fill cells.

Use commercial scenarios conditional on approval, plus a separate development-risk/timing layer. Approval can produce a narrow label or weak efficacy; model those outcomes rather than equating approval with the full base-case sales forecast. Avoid counting the same risk in PoS, eligible population, penetration and an unexplained discount-rate premium. Correlated assumptions must form coherent scenarios: better efficacy can affect approval, label, price, uptake and persistence together.

Show sensitivity to one driver at a time and joint downside/base/upside scenarios. Decompose the difference from Needham into prevalence, diagnosis, eligibility, uptake, exposure, net price, geography, launch timing and competition. Flag when an apparent peak difference is only inflation or a different peak year. Keep PoS out of conditional product sales and introduce it explicitly in valuation. Do not derive an assumed sales forecast from a desired equity value.

STANDARD OF SUCCESS

The goal is not a larger stack of anecdotes or a confident-looking PoS percentage. It is a reproducible explanation of which historical observations genuinely transfer to each CMP-002 assumption, where that transfer breaks down, and how the remaining uncertainty changes the independent forecast and valuation.

ADDITIONAL REQUIRED MODULE: POST-LOE CASH FLOWS AND TERMINAL VALUE

Audit explicit post-LOE cash flows separately from terminal value beyond the forecast horizon. Needham p.28 models 2026-2050 cash flows, 9% discount rate, 0% perpetual terminal growth, $238m terminal value at the end of the forecast and approximately $30m present terminal value. Its reported EV is $514m, equity value $649m, and terminal value share of EV 6%. Rounded annual PV cash flows for 2047-2050 are $25m/$12m/$6m/$3m, totaling $46m; including terminal value gives approximately $76m of post-2046 value (15% of EV, 12% of equity). These are arithmetic decompositions of printed rounded figures, not an independently validated DCF. Explicit cash-flow PV is reported as $485m; $485m plus $30m versus $514m EV reflects displayed rounding or unreconciled underlying precision.

Investigate why roughly 48% annual revenue erosion through 2050 changes to a 0%-growth perpetuity thereafter, while the detailed sales model continues erosion through 2053. Test continued erosion, finite runoff, a defensible residual franchise and alternative erosion speeds. Do not treat 0% growth as zero terminal value. Test LOE date shifts independently: moving expiry changes protected cash flows before 2047 and may matter substantially more than changing only the terminal growth rate. Verify patent-extension probabilities rather than assuming guaranteed extension.

Audit fixed versus variable costs, shutdown costs, working-capital release, tax effects, maintenance investment, manufacturing economics and discontinuation decisions in the tail. A zero-post-LOE-cash-flow haircut is a valuation contribution diagnostic, not a fully recomputed scenario where all revenues disappear but costs are also ignored.

Report value shares and sensitivity on four denominators: DCF enterprise value, DCF equity value, per-share DCF value and blended price target. Needham weights DCF and its separate peak-multiple valuation 50/50, so a terminal-only change affects half the combined target if the other method is held fixed. Check whether changed LOE assumptions should also change the multiple or its peak-year anchor; do not imply the multiple method is economically independent of commercial durability. Compare remaining protected life at the peak-multiple valuation date against its assumed 7x multiple, not merely against current-EV acquisition multiples. Keep single-asset runoff separate from any pipeline-reinvestment perpetuity; do not introduce unsupported future products.
