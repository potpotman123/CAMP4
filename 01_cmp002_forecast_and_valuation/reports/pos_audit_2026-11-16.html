PROJECT 1 · Independent development-risk audit · Evidence cutoff 16 November 2026 · Public-source v1

Is Needham’s 25% approval probability defensible?

Qualified answer: 25% is defensible as an optimistic judgmental assumption, but this audit does not establish it as a conservative or empirically calibrated base case. The closest pediatric protein-restoration peers are too few and too immature to identify an approval rate. Strong biological rationale and successful CNS ASOs support meaningful upside; gene-restoration and biomarker failures show why genetic causality is not enough.

Working-model recommendation: if a single number is required now, use 20% provisionally, show 10% downside, retain Needham’s 25% explicitly, and test 35% upside. These are judgmental valuation scenarios, not statistical estimates, a confidence interval, or evidence that 20% is the uniquely correct answer. The five-point difference from Needham is not statistically demonstrated. A range-only presentation is equally defensible until human data arrive.

1. Match the actual starting point

CMP-002 is a regulatory-RNA-targeting ASO intended to increase SYNGAP1 expression. As of the November company update, it had trial clearances in Australia, Argentina, the UK and EU, while first patient dosing remained planned for year-end. This is a cleared, pre-first-dose program, not discovery-stage research. UK clearance was already public before Needham’s October 15 initiation. CAMP4 Q3 2026 update; CAMP4 UK clearance.

The hypothesis has several separable layers: a causal gene deficiency; a targetable regulatory RNA; safe and sufficiently distributed drug exposure; protein restoration in relevant neurons; and measurable benefit at the treated age. Validation of the gene does not validate every later layer. CAMP’s mouse rescue is favorable drug-specific evidence, but not human proof. Independent adult mouse genetic-restoration experiments argue against assuming that all deficits are irreversibly fixed in infancy; they do not establish a human treatment window. CAMP4 Q3 10-Q; SYNGAP1 adult genetic rescue original study.

Needham applies 25% in both its DCF and multiple analysis. The reviewed report does not supply a matched historical cohort or a stage-transition derivation for that figure. It is therefore an analyst assumption to audit, not an empirically documented base rate. Needham initiation.

2. Universe construction and what it can answer

We screened a public registry query, historical ASO pipeline literature, named candidates and aliases, sponsor discontinuation disclosures and FDA approval records. The initial generic registry search returned 25 trial records; 25 named/alias searches and seven targeted registry records filled gaps. All raw responses are saved. The result is 24 core assets and 28 drug–indication pairs with identified group-trial starts in 2010–2024, plus an explicit boundary/watchlist. This is a representative reference library, not a complete global inception cohort.

Active assets have different follow-up, faster failures may resolve earlier than approvals, the set is not exhaustively sampled, and many cases share a sponsor/target/platform. 4. Supporting successes and disconfirming failures

Nusinersen establishes that increasing a deficient protein with repeated intrathecal ASO treatment can produce an approved medicine, but SMA is primarily a motor-neuron disease and the approach modifies SMN2 splicing. It is an older proof of feasibility, not a current matched approval rate. Biogen FDA approval announcement.

Zilganersen supplies a recent rare genetic CNS success: September 2026 FDA approval for Alexander disease in children and adults. It reduces GFAP rather than increasing a deficient synaptic protein. This supports delivery and rare-neurology feasibility more directly than CAMP’s precise mechanism. FDA Zanvastro approval.

Tofersen demonstrates a possible regulatory pathway using a biomarker reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit. Its accelerated approval is a regulatory success, not evidence that SYNGAP1 has an equivalent accepted surrogate. Keep accelerated approval separate from demonstrated broad functional benefit. FDA Qalsody approval.

WVE-004 and BIIB105 show why molecular target engagement cannot be treated as clinical success. Their sponsors reported target/biomarker effects without the clinical benefit needed to continue. BIIB078 adds a genetic-CNS clinical failure; WVE-120101/102 show sequence/chemistry and target-modulation risk. These are mechanistic cautions, not equally matched pediatric-restoration peers. Wave C9 program community letter; Ionis BIIB105 results; Biogen/Ionis BIIB078 results; Wave PRECISION-HD sponsor results.

Tominersen illustrates why the timeline matters: the failed 2021 pivotal trial was followed by a lower-dose study; Roche stopped HD2 in 2026 after inadequate clinical benefit. The distinct recent RG6496 program was stopped following chronic animal-toxicity findings. The latter remains outside the pre-2025 core-entry window but demonstrates that trial clearance does not eliminate later toxicology risk. Roche HD sponsor community letter.

Ulefnersen had positive Phase III FUS-ALS results in September 2026, but is still an unresolved approval outcome in this audit. Diranersen missed its Phase II primary dose-response endpoint while its sponsor planned further development. Neither belongs in a simplistic approved/failed binary. Ionis FUSION result; Biogen Q2 10-Q.

The oldest three-asset group has two approvals and one stopped asset; the newest twelve-asset group has one approval and many unresolved programs. Neither is an unbiased rate for CMP-002. Older results have longer follow-up and different technology; newer results better reflect contemporary practice but are immature. No arbitrary recency-weighted pooled percentage is fitted.

The saved five- and ten-year calendar-follow-up diagnostics illustrate timing sensitivity, not a lifetime approval estimate. A five-year cutoff falls just before some approvals. No survival model or confidence interval is fitted because the selection frame, exact event histories, status continuity and platform dependence do not support that precision.

BIO/Informa/QLS reported 7.9% Phase-I-to-approval overall and 17.0% for rare-disease programs over its historical period. These overlapping subgroup benchmarks are context only: do not multiply a rare-disease uplift by a genetic-target uplift, or pool them with our descriptive counts. BIO/Informa/QLS 2011-2020 study.

6. What would justify moving the assumption?

Closest ongoing restoration peers succeed or fail in randomized pivotal trialsUpdates the class/disease-family context, but transfer is limited by target and mechanism differences.

Assessment of 25%: possible and defensible as an optimistic assumption, not proven excessive simply because the drug is undosed. Equally, it is not established as conservative by monogenic causality or by successful ASOs elsewhere. A 20% provisional working input expresses modest caution, not a measured five-point disadvantage. Use the full scenario set and revisit at prespecified clinical milestones.

At identical conditional sales and timing, 20% versus 25% reduces the probability-weighted revenue/asset contribution by 20%. It does not automatically reduce total equity value by 20%: net cash and costs have different risk treatment. Development costs should be weighted by the probability of reaching the spending stage, not all multiplied by the eventual approval probability.

7. Needham-date assessment versus today

The September 2026 GTX-102 pivotal failure, zilganersen approval and positive FUSION result were public before October 15. These belong in the retrospective assessment. The November GTX-102 wind-down and latest pipeline updates confirm or update statuses afterward; they must not be presented as information Needham had at initiation. CAMP was already UK-cleared before the report, while later EU clearance further resolved an operational gate. Nothing here supplies human CMP-002 efficacy.

8. What remains incomplete

  1. Exact clearance-stage denominator: recover historical IND/CTA clearances, assets that never dosed, and preclinical cancellations. This would improve stage matching but is not reliably captured by clinical registries.
  2. Coverage: complete aliases and non-US registry coverage, individually adjudicate recent-entry leads and legacy SOD1 programs, and resolve BIIB094’s asset fate. A targeted paid historical pipeline dataset might help, but no paid collection was initiated.
  3. Clinical translation: independently review full CMP-002 pharmacology/toxicology and the endpoint-to-benefit bridge. Public company summaries do not disclose everything required.
  4. Statistical calibration: only after a defensible denominator and event history exist should a competing-risk or hierarchical model be considered. Current evidence cannot distinguish 20% from 25% with statistical confidence.

The outputs can enter the model now as peer evidence, failure modes and explicitly judgmental scenarios. They cannot enter as an empirically estimated CMP-002 approval probability.
