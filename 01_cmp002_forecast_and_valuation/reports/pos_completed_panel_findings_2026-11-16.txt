COMPLETED PUBLIC-SOURCE PANEL REVIEW — CMP-002 PoS AUDIT
16 November 2026 | Project 1

Decision
The finite-panel review is now complete and actual approval-horizon calculations are available. It does NOT establish a new, independently calibrated lifetime PoS for CMP-002. Needham's 25% is not contradicted by these broad historical benchmarks, but the closest mechanisms remain too immature to validate it. Keep 25% as an explicitly judgmental scenario while separately assessing CMP-002's platform, target, dose, safety, efficacy and regulatory risks. Do not average overlapping peer-set percentages into a supposedly independent estimate.

What was completed
All 449 previously retained or entry-uncertain histories received the second review: 42 ALS, 107 other neurological, 122 metabolic and 178 systemic. There are no untouched assigned rows. The 518-row mapped inventory now contains 316 included drug-indication records, 159 exclusions and 43 unresolved identity/entry cases. The 316 records reduce to 310 asset/combination clusters; 242 records, or 238 clusters, have the stricter documented-entry qualification. The other included records retain explicitly labeled first-observed registry proxies.

“Reviewed” does not mean “every historical fact recovered.” The remaining 43 entry cases have searches and reasons, and are included in a separate hypothetical entry sensitivity. Uncertain development fate also remains in some qualified histories. That is different from an untouched research queue and from being a clinical failure.

The previous 418 retained / 333 unresolved presentation mixed three questions: whether a record belonged in the cohort, whether development continued, and whether FDA approval occurred. That made the preliminary inventory unsuitable as a finished PoS analysis. The revised ledger separates these questions.

Actual calculations
Endpoint: FDA approval in the target/original qualifying indication within H years of first human exposure. A later disease indication does not count. Discontinued, ongoing and development-fate-unknown assets remain in the denominator; no observed approval within H is not called a lifetime failure. Only assets definitely old enough for each horizon enter its main denominator. US approval is checked separately from development status using researched aliases and FDA inventories.

Numerator ranges reflect uncertain first-dose dates, not confidence intervals. Each horizon has a different mature denominator. These columns are not a single cohort's cumulative curve. The four closest peers are also the entire current intrathecal-restoration/neurodevelopment filter, so those two cuts do not supply independent evidence.

The 10-year genetic-medicine successes are elivaldogene autotemcel, nusinersen and onasemnogene abeparvovec. Two are in SMA: three successes do not represent three independent validations of new CNS disease biology. The broader CNS numerator also includes cerliponase alfa and deutetrabenazine; the latter illustrates why this is broader than a new-platform, new-target CMP-002 comparator set.

Atidarsagene remains in the 10-year denominator even though it eventually received FDA approval: its approval came after that horizon. This is a concrete reason not to interpret the unapproved-at-10-years share as the lifetime failure rate. Tofersen, risdiplam and UX111 are among date-boundary cases whose treatment changes under the explicitly saved maturity sensitivity.

Sensitivity and recency
For the broad panel's 10-year horizon:
- Stricter documented-entry subset: 17/55 = 30.9%.
- Reviewed-entry panel including explicit entry proxies: 21/74 = 28.4%.
- Hypothetically include unresolved-entry cases at their frozen registry-entry proxies: 21/86 = 24.4%. This assumes those cases qualify; it does not resolve their identity or dosing history.
- Assume every entry-date maturity-boundary case is old enough: reviewed panel becomes 101 observations with 26.7–27.7% approved within 10 years, depending on dosing date.
- Latest-component rather than earliest-component clock for shared combination clusters: 20/72 = 27.8%.

These are alternative assumptions, not observations to blend. The spread is useful for auditing the denominator but is not a statistical confidence interval and does not capture entirely missing programs.

Restricting entry to 2015–2020 gives an 8-year broad-panel result of 9–13/77 (11.7–16.9%); genetic CNS is 2/19 (10.5%); genetic CNS genetic medicines are 1/7 (14.3%). The newer genetic-medicine group has only ONE definitely 10-year-mature observation. That is why an apparently precise “modern 10-year rate” would be misleading. Recent failures and progress remain valuable case evidence; recent programs cannot supply long follow-up that has not elapsed.

Closest CMP-002 peers
Zorevunersen, apazunersen, rugonersen and obudanersen remain the four closest mechanistic/clinical analogues. They supply one observed discontinuation and three ongoing programs, with no FDA approvals by cutoff. Three are definitely five-year-mature under the stored entry intervals; none is eight-year-mature. Their 0/3 five-year result is NOT a 0% eventual PoS. Three Angelman programs also share disease biology and target-related risks, further limiting effective independent evidence.

Material historical corrections
- Prior human exposure excluded later-indication records, including HYNR-CS's pre-2010 pilot, IC14 and several repurposed drugs.
- First dosing followed registry start materially for QRL-201, BEAM-101, nula-cel, EB-101, SGT001 and others. BEAM301 first dosed in 2025 and is outside the 2010–2024 dosed cohort.
- Ciprocopan's Chinese approval is not an FDA success. Pozelimab's CHAPLE approval and zilucoplan's gMG approval do not rescue their PNH rows.
- nula-cel, SP-420 and PM359 continued after apparent stops; AT845 remains active. Trial status alone was insufficient.
- BB305's first qualifying approval is beti-cel in 2022, not the later lovo-cel SCD approval. Shared-product event provenance is retained in each horizon's approval_event_sources.

What can enter the valuation model
The measured horizon rates and their sensitivities can serve as historical calibration checks. They show that 25% is not obviously excessive solely because a candidate is a rare genetic/CNS therapy. They do not prove 25% is correct for CMP-002, and they do not justify replacing it with 28% or 30%.

CMP-002's pre-dose landmark remains earlier than the main dosed cohort. Clean registered-but-never-dosed programs are retained separately; those observations are not a census of all preclinical attrition. Human ASO delivery experience does not establish regRNA-platform efficacy, SYNGAP1 target translation, developmental reversibility, therapeutic window or pivotal endpoint success. A literal human-naive-target filter remains unavailable where historical target evidence is missing. These issues require case-level judgment or a genuinely broader historical development database, not an arbitrary numerical haircut or nested-rate average.

Limitations that remain after completing the work
This is a public-registry panel discovered through 48 selected rare-disease terms, not all rare drugs or all drugs. Missing unregistered/discontinued programs, geography, discovery terms and commercial/academic development differences can bias coverage. Requiring stronger historical documentation can itself select better-documented successes. A no-match FDA alias screen is an operational negative finding; undiscovered aliases and historical CBER coverage gaps remain possible. These limitations prevent a claim that the calculated fractions are an unbiased population PoS estimator. Arithmetic checks and independent review do not remove selection bias.
