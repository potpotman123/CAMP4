Examples: broad rare includes systemic metabolic, neuromuscular and other disease programmes; genetic CNS includes small molecules as well as genetic medicines. Genetic medicines include viral vectors, ex-vivo gene therapy and RNA-directed drugs; they are not synonymous with ASOs.
All 18 ASOs in this selected genetic-CNS panel are intrathecally delivered, so route is not an additional numerical narrowing here. The six deficient-protein-restoration ASOs are nusinersen, salanersen, zorevunersen, apazunersen, obudanersen and rugonersen.
The four closest biological/clinical-rationale peers are zorevunersen–Dravet and apazunersen, obudanersen, rugonersen–Angelman. The SMA therapies leave at this final phenotype filter, not because Spinraza is irrelevant to ASO delivery or protein restoration. Three Angelman assets share target/disease risks; four assets are not four fully independent biological experiments.
The 18-ASO estimate of 31.3% at ten years is fragile: only two unapproved observations extend that far; the descriptive bootstrap range is approximately 0–64%. It is not a reliable 31% prior for CMP-002. The six- and four-member sets have no supported ten-year estimate. No approval yet in the closest four does not mean a 0% eventual PoS.

RECENCY, UNCERTAINTY AND NEEDHAM 25%
Restricting entry to 2015 onward changes ten-year estimates to 20.7% broad rare, 15.5% genetic CNS, 10.4% genetic medicines within genetic CNS, and 28.0% intrathecal ASO. These tails still depend on the earliest entrants in that restricted era; they do not measure ten-year outcomes for drugs first dosed recently.
The broad ten-year descriptive bootstrap interval is approximately 15–28%; genetic CNS 9–32%; genetic CNS medicines 4–40%. These resampling ranges describe this selected panel, not population-valid confidence intervals.
Needham 25% is neither independently validated nor ruled out. It sits near the broad 12-year result, but comparing a pre-dose lifetime assumption with post-entry finite-horizon curves is not an apples-to-apples calibration. Delivery precedent, target biology, clinical translatability, endpoint sensitivity and pre-dose attrition still require judgment. Do not average nested percentages as though independent estimates.
Status-only corrections do not change these curves: failed candidates remain known nonapprovals through the administrative cutoff. The two added nonapproved entrants modestly change the denominator/risk sets. Unknowns are retained. Deleting them or censoring failures at discontinuation would introduce avoidable bias.

DRUG–INDICATION PAIRS: THE CORRECT NEXT DATA MODEL
For the question whether CMP-002 will be approved in SYNGAP1, the natural unit is a molecule–indication pair. The current panel instead consolidates molecules with selected indication records. It must not be relabelled a comprehensive pair panel.
Examples needing separation: VO659 in Huntington and SCA; soticlestat in Dravet and Lennox–Gastaut; BMB-101 in those two epilepsy syndromes; BB305 sickle-cell and beta-thalassemia constructs/labels require identity adjudication; HepaStem has a mixed UCD/Crigler-Najjar entry. In contrast, duplicate CF combination records are not distinct indication bets.
A pair table needs its own clinical-entry evidence, target-indication FDA outcome and decision history, molecule/construct ID, and prior human-data/approval status at entry. Healthy-volunteer Phase I may support several later indications; specify whether the clock starts at molecule first dosing or disease-specific entry and run both when feasible.
A later indication approval never rescues the original pair. The same indication in a new territory is not a new disease sample. Separate paediatric/age/genotype cohorts only when the prespecified indication taxonomy justifies it. Trial counts are not sample counts.
Pairs sharing a molecule are correlated. Report unweighted pair outcomes, cluster-resampled uncertainty by molecule, and equal-total-weight-per-molecule sensitivity. Flag prior approval separately, since an indication expansion of an established drug is a different risk proposition from a first-in-human unapproved CNS medicine.
Future genuinely nested funnel: all qualifying clinical-entry pairs → previously unapproved molecules at pair entry → rare disease → genetic CNS disease → ASO → intrathecal → deficient-protein restoration → closest neurodevelopmental phenotype. The current data support only the selected-molecule funnel shown above. Building the all-drugs and expanded-indication top requires further collection, not arithmetic relabelling.

This bounded discovery pass is complete. Unresolved entries require stronger programme/construct evidence or a historical indication-level export. No inference from a missing search hit, an outdated registry, a completed study or a corporate acquisition alone was promoted to a confirmed failure.
Frozen prior source files and calculations are preserved. Source retrieval dates can postdate the comparison cutoff; evidence is used to describe pre-cutoff events. This is a current retrospective audit, not a point-in-time backtest.
Not a probability sample or complete census; discovery remains concentrated in genetic CNS programmes.
Clinical-entry clock may begin in healthy volunteers before disease-specific patient exposure. Pair-level clocks require separate adjudication.
Historical label consistency and inherited approval/entry ascertainment remain limitations.
Recent entrants have immature follow-up; 10/12-year curves disproportionately depend on older entrants and an exchangeability assumption.
Active includes retained programmes and safety follow-up; it does not universally mean advancing toward approval.
Failure counts include sponsor/portfolio exits, not only biological failures.
Finite-horizon invariance to status labels is intentional: administrative censoring depends on dates/approvals, not active/failed classification.
No valid independent weighted blend of nested layers; their samples overlap.
No efficacy or eventual approval rate can be estimated reliably from the four closest peers alone.
